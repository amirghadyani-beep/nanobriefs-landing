Molecular crystal memristors Nano Electronic
Qin, L., Guan, P., Shao, J. et al. · Nature Nanotechnology, 2025, Vol. 20, pp. 1641–1647 · DOI: 10.1038/s41565-025-02013-z

A molecular-crystal channel material lets ions migrate through intermolecular spaces without disrupting the crystal structure, preserving device integrity across extensive switching cycles — unlike conventional filamentary memristors.

Switching energy: ~26 zJ/op  |  Endurance: >10⁹ cycles
High-On/Off-Ratio Vertical Transistors with Defect-Engineered MoSe₂ and van der Waals VSe₂ Contacts Nano Electronic
Choi, D.E. et al. · ACS Nano, Oct 2025, 19(40), pp. 35601–35608 · DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.5c10639

Defect engineering of MoSe₂ combined with van der Waals VSe₂ contacts suppresses leakage current through gate-field-free regions, targeting a structural limitation that has held back vertical 2D transistor scaling.

On/off ratio: reported as high — exact figure flagged for verification against full text.
Three-dimensional photonic integration for ultra-low-energy, high-bandwidth interchip data links Photonic
Nature Photonics, 21 Mar 2025 · DOI: 10.1038/s41566-025-01633-0

A 3D-integrated photonic-electronic interface targets the interconnect bottleneck in AI accelerator and data-center architectures, where electrical I/O is becoming an energy and bandwidth limiter.

800 Gb/s across 80 channels  |  Tx: 50 fJ/bit  |  Rx: 70 fJ/bit

Feng, X. et al. · Nature Photonics, 2025 · DOI: 10.1038/s41566-024-01579-9

Reframes optical loss — normally something to avoid — as a usable design resource, demonstrating a hybrid silicon photonic switch built around non-Hermitian (gain/loss-engineered) dynamics.

Insertion-loss / switching-speed figures flagged for verification against full text.
Single-Qubit Gates with Errors at the 10⁻⁷ Level Computational
Smith, M.C., Leu, A.D., Miyanishi, K., Gely, M.F., Lucas, D.M. · Physical Review Letters, Jun 2025, Vol. 134, 230601 · DOI: 10.1103/42w2-6ccy

A trapped-ion ⁴³Ca⁺ qubit achieves a new record for single-qubit gate fidelity, at room temperature and without magnetic shielding, using electronic rather than laser control — simplifying the engineering path toward scalable ion-trap chips.

Gate error: 1.5(4) × 10⁻⁷ per Clifford gate  |  Gate time: 4.4–35 µs
