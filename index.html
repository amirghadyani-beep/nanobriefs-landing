Molecular crystal memristors Nano Electronic
Qin, L., Guan, P., Shao, J. et al. · Nature Nanotechnology, 2025, Vol. 20, pp. 1641–1647 · DOI: 10.1038/s41565-025-02013-z

Research Question

Can a memristor channel material be found that resists the structural degradation which limits the energy efficiency and endurance of existing resistive-switching devices?

Key Findings

  • A molecular-crystal channel material (Sb₂O₃, with cage-like units linked by van der Waals forces) lets ions migrate through intermolecular spaces without disrupting the crystal structure.
  • This preserves device integrity across extensive switching cycles, unlike conventional filamentary memristors.
  • The device supports both non-volatile and volatile resistive switching, reconfigurable across scales from micrometres to nanometres.

Nano-Metrics

Switching energy: ~26 zJ per operation  |  Endurance: >10⁹ switching cycles

Limitations

The abstract does not report integration density or yield across large arrays, which matters for practical neuromorphic hardware at scale.

Practical Implications

Ultra-low switching energy combined with high endurance is directly relevant to in-memory computing hardware for neuromorphic AI accelerators.

Relevance: 9/10
High-On/Off-Ratio Vertical Transistors with Defect-Engineered MoSe₂ and van der Waals VSe₂ Contacts Nano Electronic
Choi, D.E., Park, H., Choi, H., Choi, S.Y., Kang, B., Kim, H.H. · ACS Nano, 14 Oct 2025, 19(40), pp. 35601–35608 · DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.5c10639

Research Question

Can leakage current through gate-field-free regions in 2D vertical field-effect transistors (VFETs) be suppressed to unlock sub-nanometer channel lengths?

Key Findings

  • Defect engineering of MoSe₂ combined with van der Waals VSe₂ contacts suppresses leakage current through gate-field-free regions under zero gate bias.
  • The design achieves a high on/off current ratio in an n-channel MoSe₂ VFET architecture.
  • The approach targets a structural limitation (rather than a materials-purity limitation) that has held back vertical 2D transistor performance.

Nano-Metrics

On/off current ratio: reported as high, exact figure not captured in the publicly available abstract — flagged for follow-up against the full text.

Per our verification policy, we do not invent numbers our pipeline hasn't confirmed against the source.

Limitations

Vertical channel lengths below one nanometer raise open questions about reproducibility and yield that a single-device demonstration does not resolve.

Practical Implications

Sub-nanometer vertical channels could extend transistor scaling past the limits of conventional planar 2D FETs.

Relevance: 7/10
Three-dimensional photonic integration for ultra-low-energy, high-bandwidth interchip data links Photonic
Nature Photonics, 21 Mar 2025 · DOI: 10.1038/s41566-025-01633-0

Research Question

Can photonics and electronics be integrated in three dimensions to deliver a chip-to-chip data interface that is both energy-efficient and dense enough for AI-era data centers?

Key Findings

  • A 3D-integrated photonic-electronic interface achieves an 800 Gb/s aggregate data rate across 80 communication channels.
  • Energy consumption reaches tens of femtojoules per transmitted bit — well below typical chip-scale transceiver energy budgets.
  • Transmitter and receiver front ends individually achieve 50 fJ/bit and 70 fJ/bit respectively, at 10 Gb/s per channel.

Nano-Metrics

Aggregate bandwidth: 800 Gb/s  |  Channels: 80  |  Tx energy: 50 fJ/bit  |  Rx energy: 70 fJ/bit

Limitations

The abstract does not detail manufacturing yield or cost at scale for the 3D bonding process used.

Practical Implications

Directly targets the interconnect bottleneck in AI accelerator and data-center architectures, where electrical I/O is becoming an energy and bandwidth limiter.

Relevance: 9/10
Non-Hermitian hybrid silicon photonic switching Photonic
Feng, X., Wu, T., Gao, Z., Zhao, H., Wu, S., Zhang, Y., Ge, L., Feng, L. · Nature Photonics, 2025 · DOI: 10.1038/s41566-024-01579-9

Research Question

Can non-Hermitian physics — where a system's gain and loss are engineered together rather than avoided — be used as a design principle for compact, high-performance silicon photonic switches?

Key Findings

  • The team demonstrates a hybrid silicon photonic switch whose operation is built around non-Hermitian (gain/loss-engineered) dynamics rather than conventional loss-avoidant design.
  • This reframes a previously undesirable optical effect (loss) as a usable design resource for switching.

Relevant to next-generation optical switching fabrics for data-center and AI interconnects, an area of active silicon-photonics competition.

Single-Qubit Gates with Errors at the 10⁻⁷ Level Computational
Smith, M.C., Leu, A.D., Miyanishi, K., Gely, M.F., Lucas, D.M. · Physical Review Letters, 12 Jun 2025, Vol. 134, 230601 · DOI: 10.1103/42w2-6ccy

Research Question

How low can single-qubit gate error rates be pushed in a trapped-ion platform using practical, scalable (electronic rather than laser) control?

Key Findings

  • A trapped-ion ⁴³Ca⁺ hyperfine clock qubit achieves single-qubit gate errors at the sub-part-per-million level.
  • Minimum benchmarked error per Clifford gate: 1.5(4) × 10⁻⁷ — a new record for any quantum computing platform.
  • Achieved at room temperature, without magnetic shielding, using electronic (not laser) control — simplifying the engineering requirements relative to laser-driven approaches.

Nano-Metrics

Gate error: 1.5(4) × 10⁻⁷ per Clifford gate  |  Gate time range: 4.4–35 µs  |  Qubit: trapped ⁴³Ca⁺ ion

Limitations

The result is for a single qubit; two-qubit entangling gates — needed for full computation — remain roughly three orders of magnitude less accurate (~99.9% fidelity in the best current demonstrations).

Practical Implications

Electronic (non-laser) control at this fidelity is cheaper and easier to scale in ion-trap chips, a meaningful step toward practical fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Relevance: 10/10
